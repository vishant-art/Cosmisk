PART 4: WHO AND WHAT IS IN THE AD
===============================================================================

Q20. Who approves the person who appears in our ads?
     NOW: nobody. Whoever types the description gets that person. Typing
          "beautiful indian woman" created a character used through the whole
          ad. There are no rules on age, ethnicity or appearance, and the client
          never sees the person before we make the ad.

Q21. Should every brand use the same narrator voice?
     NOW: yes, one default voice reads every ad for every client.

Q22. Which product do we advertise?
     NOW: automatically the best-selling product by revenue.
     WHY: the bestseller needs the least help. Should the client choose, or
          should we push new or slow-moving stock?

Q23. One product per ad, or several?
     NOW: one.

Q24. Should we show prices and discounts in ads?
     NOW: the price and any discount tags are fed in, so they can end up in the
          wording.

Q25. Should ads automatically tie into festivals and seasons?
     NOW: only by accident, if the product happens to be tagged that way.

Q26. Who checks that the claims in our ad copy are true and legal?
     NOW: nobody. Wording like "best seller" or "up to 50% off" comes straight
          from the store's own product tags into the ad.
     WHY: a wrong discount claim is a real problem and no human is checking.

Q27. Are we allowed to invent a brand?
     NOW: yes. If the client gives us no brand details, the system invents a
          brand name, slogan, colours, fonts and a logo idea.


===============================================================================
PART 5: OUR QUALITY BAR
===============================================================================

Q28. If our own quality check fails an ad, do we still deliver it?
     NOW: yes, delivered with a "did not pass" flag. It used to be thrown away,
          which meant we paid for it and gave the client nothing.

Q29. Which problems should actually stop an ad from going out?
     NOW: any ONE of these blocks it, all treated as equally bad: unreadable
          text, product missing, distorted face, extra arm or hand, captions
          covering something, unreadable captions, shots that do not flow, a
          frozen shot, or the person changing between shots.
     REAL EXAMPLE: our most recent ad was marked failed ONLY because a caption
          sat on top of the dress. Everything else passed and the ad was good.

Q30. How many times do we redo a bad image before shipping or dropping it?
     NOW: once.

Q31. Should we pay for an AI reviewer to check every single image?
     NOW: yes, on by default. Small cost per image.

Q32. Do we show clients the versions we rejected?
     NOW: no, recorded internally but not shown.

Q33. What does the client see if a job fails completely?
     NOW: an error. There is no friendly fallback.


===============================================================================
PART 6: WHAT WE LEARN FROM
===============================================================================

Q34. Should we judge an ad on attention or on sales?
     NOW: attention only. The system learns from whether people stopped
          scrolling in the first couple of seconds. It ignores revenue.
     WHY: attention is what the creative controls, but the client wants sales.

Q35. How much proof before past results change what we make next?
     NOW: a version needs 1,000 views and 95% confidence before it influences
          anything. Anything weaker is recorded as "not proven" and ignored.
     WHY: a high bar means we are never fooled by luck, but we learn slowly.

Q36. Should we study the client's failed ads too, not just the winners?
     NOW: yes, we look at the top 12 and the bottom 5.

Q37. How much must an old ad have spent before we treat its result as real?
     NOW: $100.

Q38. Which past campaigns do we learn from?
     NOW: the ones with the best return, by default.

Q39. What are we allowed to test one against the other?
     NOW: only three things: the opening line, the caption style, and the look.
     WHY: keeps results meaningful, but limits what we can experiment with.


===============================================================================
PART 7: LEGAL, DATA AND TRUST
===============================================================================

Q40. Do we label our ads as AI-generated?
     NOW: no, nothing is labelled. Meta has required this since March 2026 and
          everything we make falls under it.
     WHY: this is a legal gap, not a preference. NEEDS AN OWNER NOW.

Q41. What client information are we comfortable sending to outside AI companies?
     NOW: the client's real product photos, descriptions, prices, and their ad
          performance figures.
     WHY: should be in the client contract.

Q42. How long do we keep the ads and client images we make?
     NOW: forever. Nothing expires.

Q43. Can a client ask us to delete everything?
     NOW: there is no process for it.

Q44. Can anything go live on Meta without a human?
     NOW: no, and that is deliberate. A person has to publish it.
     WHY: worth confirming this stays a permanent promise we can sell.

Q45. Who inside our company is allowed to spend money on making ads?
     NOW: anyone with access. There are no roles or limits.
