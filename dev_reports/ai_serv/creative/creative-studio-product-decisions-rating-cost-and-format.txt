PART 2: MONEY
===============================================================================
Right now the only thing stopping us spending is whether the vendor account has
money left.

Q1. When someone asks for photo ads, should we also make a video automatically?
    NOW: yes. Every photo-ad job also makes a video clip, about $1.22, with
         nobody approving it. If the account is low, it quietly skips it.
    WHY: people get charged for something they did not ask for.

Q2. What is the most we will spend on one ad?
    NOW: there is no limit. The only check is whether the account can cover it.
         A normal ad with 3 clips costs about $4.50. The system is allowed to
         plan up to 12 clips, which is about $15.
    WHY: one person clicking the wrong thing can spend a lot.

Q3. What is the monthly budget per client, and who approves going over it?
    NOW: nobody has set one.

Q4. How many versions do we make each time?
    NOW: 4 different ad ideas, each in 4 sizes = 16 images per job.
    WHY: more choice for the client, but more cost and more for us to review.

Q5. How hard do we try to fix a bad clip before giving up?
    NOW: try again once, then reword it, then try a different shot, then give
         up on that shot. Hard stop at 40 attempts for one ad, about $48.

Q6. If a client asks for a small change, do they pay for the whole ad again?
    NOW: no. Anything unchanged is reused for free. Only the parts that change
         cost money.
    WHY: this is a good selling point and should shape how we price revisions.

Q7. What turnaround do we promise?
    NOW: a full video ad takes roughly 15 minutes to produce end to end.


===============================================================================
PART 3: HOW THE AD LOOKS AND SOUNDS
===============================================================================

Q8.  Should the client's logo appear on the ad?
     NOW: no logo appears at all. We never put one on.
     WHY: most brands expect their logo on their ads.

Q9.  Should ads have background music?
     NOW: no music. There is a voiceover and small sound effects only.

Q10. Does every ad need a voiceover?
     NOW: yes, every ad gets one automatically.

Q11. Should every ad have subtitles burned into the video?
     NOW: yes, always, showing 1 to 3 words at a time.
     WHY: most people watch with sound off, so this is probably right. Confirm.

Q12. Should ads have sound effects?
     NOW: yes, added automatically.

Q13. Do we only make vertical (phone-shaped) videos?
     NOW: yes, vertical only by default.
     WHY: rules out some placements unless we change it.

Q14. What quality do videos go out in?
     NOW: standard (720p), not high definition.
     WHY: higher quality costs more. Is standard good enough for a paying client?

Q15. How long should a standard ad be?
     NOW: 20 seconds by default. It can be 6 to 90 seconds.

Q16. How fast should the ad cut between shots?
     NOW: a new shot every 1 to 4 seconds, up to 12 shots.

Q17. How long can the headline be?
     NOW: 6 words maximum for the headline, 1 to 3 words for the button text.

Q18. Should our ads look homemade or polished?
     NOW: homemade on purpose. Shaky camera, ordinary lighting, slightly
          off-centre framing, with a bit of wobble and grain added deliberately
          so it looks like a real person filmed it. A polished premium look
          exists but is not the default.
     WHY: this is a brand positioning choice, currently made for every client.

Q19. What languages do we support?
     NOW: English only, even when the store and prices are in another market.
